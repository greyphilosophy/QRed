Privacy & data handling

Privacy Policy

How QRed handles document contents, account identity, and temporary technical data.

Last updated: October 26, 2026

1. What QRed does

QRed helps people seal documents with QR codes and cryptographic signatures, and check those seals later. QRed is designed to process original PDF files, page images, extracted text, and locally held signing keys on the user's device. Normal browser sealing and verification workflows do not send original document files to QRed's server.

Account-backed sealing is optional. It connects to an external identity provider and QRed's Cloudflare Worker to request a signed document attestation. It does not require uploading the original PDF or its text to that Worker. See the security and data-flow overview.

2. Information involved in QRed

Documents and local key material

The browser opens and processes files that you select. QRed's current implementation does not operate a user-document storage service, and it does not send your local private signing key to the QRed Worker. You decide where to save, print, and share the finished document.

Account-backed attestations

When you select an available account provider (Google, Microsoft, or Amazon), QRed uses that provider to authenticate the account. Depending on provider and account, QRed receives a provider account identifier, profile name, and email address, as well as limited authentication metadata.

For the document attestation, the browser creates SHA-256 page commitments and sends these derived values to the QRed Worker for signing. The Worker does not receive PDF bytes, filenames, extracted document text, or raw document roots through this workflow. Page commitments are not salted: QRed's server or anyone with access to its request data could recognize identical page commitments or test guesses about simple, known documents. These derived values are document-related metadata, not anonymized data.

Sessions and operational information

QRed uses short-lived, encrypted, secure browser cookies during account sign-in. The current design has no permanent QRed user account database; authentication sessions expire five minutes after they begin. Site delivery and API requests necessarily expose ordinary connection information (such as network addresses, timestamps, and request metadata) to the hosting infrastructure, which may retain operational, security, or diagnostic logs under its own settings and policies.

3. What appears in a seal

A QRed-readable QR payload can carry signed document text or page evidence and associated metadata. Account-backed seals include the provider name, profile name, email address, provider-scoped account identifier, and attestation time. These details may be read by anybody who receives a copy of the sealed document and uses a compatible decoder. They are not a private repository or an encrypted container for document contents.

Consider confidentiality before sharing: a valid seal does not prevent recipients from copying the page, reading its content, or redistributing the seal and its embedded data.

4. Third-party services

QRed's site and optional Worker API run on Cloudflare infrastructure. An identity provider you choose processes its normal sign-in request and identity information under its own terms and privacy policy. QRed's document-processing page does not load a provider's JavaScript login SDK.

The browser may download application code, fonts or OCR model resources from qred.org when they are not yet available locally. Offline verification is possible once its required resources have been loaded and the relevant trust keys are available.

5. Retention and deletion

QRed does not currently maintain a database of individual sign-ins or stored PDFs. Encrypted sign-in cookies expire and are cleared when possible on completion or cancellation. Hosting-layer logs may exist independently and have separate retention controls. A completed seal remains with the document wherever its holder stores or distributes it; QRed cannot remotely retrieve or delete those copies.

For account permissions, deletion requests, and the limits of deletion, see Data deletion instructions.

6. Security and regulated information

QRed aims to minimize unnecessary document transfer, but no technical design guarantees safety or suitability in all environments. It is not represented as approved, certified, or compliant for criminal justice information (CJIS), HIPAA, FERPA, or any other specific regulated use. Organizations must assess applicable controls, device security, hosting, identity-provider use, and their own procedures before processing regulated data.

7. Changes and questions

This notice may be revised as the service changes. We will update its date when we materially revise it. You can review the public QRed source code and tests, but code and automated tests do not amount to a security certification or contractual assurance.

For privacy requests, email greyphilosophy@gmail.com. Please do not attach sensitive documents, passwords, or private keys to your request.
