1. About QRed

QRed is a freely accessible, experimental document sealing and verification tool. The website lets users create and examine QR-encoded cryptographic evidence, optionally with an identity-provider-backed attestation. People may use it voluntarily and should decide for themselves whether it works for their particular purpose.

The public reference implementation is open source under its repository license (currently Apache License 2.0); components and dependencies may have their own licenses. Those licenses govern your use of their respective source code, separately from the website service described here.

2. Your evaluation and responsibilities

You are responsible for testing your particular documents, paper sizes, printers, cameras, scanning conditions, recipients, and threat models before relying on QRed. Keep independent copies of important files and keys and maintain your own records-management and recovery procedures.

In particular, evaluate whether a seal actually covers the content and changes you care about. A QR signature authenticates its signed payload; it is not a blanket guarantee that every visible pixel, handwritten mark, graphic, stamp, or later physical alteration will be detected. Lossy visual references and OCR are inherently imperfect.

Do not assume a valid QRed seal establishes legal identity, authority to sign on behalf of an organization, accuracy of statements in the document, authenticity of the document's source, or legal admissibility.
